# Dogify pitch deck: research evidence

Compiled 2026-09-27. Every item below comes from a page or PDF that was opened and checked for the quoted number. Where only a secondary page could be opened (a university news story, a vet body page, or a press release), the item says so. Confidence tags:
- **strong**: peer-reviewed, decent sample, number read directly in the paper
- **medium**: peer-reviewed but small or narrow, or a vet body / reputable report, or a secondary source quoting a paper
- **weak**: brand-commissioned survey, single case, or a market report with unclear method

---

## 1. Problem size

### 1.1 Up to half of young dogs show distress when left alone
- **Deck line:** 46.9% of 6-month-old puppies showed separation-related behaviour when left alone.
- **Numbers:** 68 of 145 puppies (46.9%). Most common signs: pacing 14.5%, whining 7.6%, spinning or circling 6.9%.
- **Source:** Dale FC, Burn CC, Murray J, Casey R. "Canine separation-related behaviour at six months of age: Dog, owner and early-life risk factors identified using the 'Generation Pup' longitudinal study." *Animal Welfare*, 2024.
- **URL:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11655275/
- **Proof:** "Sixty-eight of the puppies (46.9%) displayed SRBs at six months of age according to the case definition." "The most commonly reported SRB was pacing (14.5%), followed by whining (7.6%) and spinning/circling (6.9%)."
- **Confidence:** medium (peer-reviewed and longitudinal, but n=145, UK, and based on what owners reported)

### 1.2 Prevalence in adult dogs: reviews put it at 14 to 20%, and a wider definition gives 22 to 55%
- **Deck line:** 14 to 20% of pet dogs have separation anxiety; one of the most common dog behaviour disorders.
- **Numbers:** 14 to 20% of the pet dog population (Pankratz et al. 2021, which cites Ballantyne 2018 and Bamberger & Houpt 2006). de Assis et al. 2020 give 22.3 to 55% showing signs, and 14 to 40% of behaviour referral cases.
- **Sources:**
  - Pankratz KE, Korman JF, Emke C, Johnson L, Griffith EH, Gruen ME. "Randomized, Placebo-Controlled Prospective Clinical Trial Evaluating the Efficacy of the Assisi Anti-anxiety Device (Calmer Canine) for the Treatment of Canine Separation Anxiety." *Frontiers in Veterinary Science*, 2021. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8720769/
  - de Assis LS, Matos R, Pike TW, Burman OHP, Mills DS. "Developing Diagnostic Frameworks in Veterinary Behavioral Medicine: Disambiguating Separation Related Problems in Dogs." *Frontiers in Veterinary Science*, 2020 (n=2,757 dogs). https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6978995/
- **Proof:** Pankratz: "Separation anxiety (SA) in dogs is a common behavioral disorder, affecting between 14 and 20% of the pet dog population." de Assis: "Between 22.3–55% of the general dog population are believed to show these signs" and "they make up between 14 and 40% of dog behavior referral cases."
- **Confidence:** strong for 14 to 20% (a range repeated across the literature). Treat 55% as the upper bound of a broad definition.

### 1.3 Large-sample benchmark (Salonen 2020): severe cases are rarer
- **Deck line:** 72.5% of 13,715 dogs had at least one highly problematic anxiety-related behaviour.
- **Numbers:** n=13,715 dogs across 264 breeds (Finland). 72.5% had some highly problematic behaviour. Noise sensitivity 32%, fear 29%. *Highly problematic* separation-related behaviour was 5%, one of the rarest traits.
- **Source:** Salonen M, Sulkama S, Mikkola S, Puurunen J, Hakanen E, Tiira K, Araujo C, Lohi H. "Prevalence, comorbidity, and breed differences in canine anxiety in 13,700 Finnish pet dogs." *Scientific Reports*, 2020.
- **URL:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7058607/
- **Proof:** "In total, 72.5% of dogs had some kind of highly problematic behaviour. Noise sensitivity was the most common anxiety trait with 32% of dogs being highly fearful of at least one noise... Separation related behaviour and aggression were the most uncommon traits with prevalences of 5% and 14%, respectively." The paper also cites earlier work: "separation anxiety occurs in 14–20% of dogs."
- **Confidence:** strong
- **Deck warning:** don't write "Salonen: X% have separation anxiety" with a large number. Salonen's own figure for highly problematic separation behaviour is 5%. Use 72.5% for general anxiety and use 14 to 20% (item 1.2) for separation anxiety.

### 1.4 Dogs left alone 5+ hours a day (UK, PDSA PAW Report)
- **Deck line:** 19% of UK dogs are left alone five or more hours on a typical weekday.
- **Numbers:** 19% in 2024 (15% in 2022, 24% in 2018). 26% are never left alone. 11% of dogs show signs of distress when left alone.
- **Source:** PDSA Animal Wellbeing (PAW) Report 2024, Dogs section. PDSA (UK veterinary charity), 2024.
- **URL:** https://www.pdsa.org.uk/what-we-do/pdsa-animal-wellbeing-report/paw-report-2024/dogs
- **Proof:** "In 2024, 19% of dogs are left alone for five or more hours, significantly more than in 2022 (15%)... This could be because fewer people are still working entirely from home." "11% of dogs show signs of distress when left alone."
- **Confidence:** strong (national vet-charity survey). Note: UK only, and dogs left alone longer showed *fewer* distress signs (8% vs 12%). PDSA suggests owners of distressed dogs may avoid leaving them at all.

### 1.5 The post-COVID return to work created new cases
- **Deck line:** 1 in 10 UK dogs developed new separation problems as lockdown hours alone changed.
- **Numbers:** 9.9% of previously unaffected dogs developed new signs by October 2020. The dogs whose time alone fell the most during lockdown were at greatest risk. n=1,807 dogs.
- **Source:** Harvey ND, Christley RM, Giragosian K, Mead R, Murray JK, Samet L, Upjohn MM, Casey RA. "Impact of Changes in Time Left Alone on Separation-Related Behaviour in UK Pet Dogs." *Animals* (MDPI), 2022.
- **URL:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8868415/
- **Proof:** "Following changes to leaving hours due to pandemic restrictions, one in ten dogs (9.9%) developed new signs of SRB by the follow-up survey in October 2020, with dogs whose leaving hours decreased most during lockdown restrictions being at increased risk of developing new signs of SRB."
- **Confidence:** strong

### 1.6 Vet-behaviourist view: most dogs show some distress at some point
- **Deck line:** At least 50% of dogs will show some separation distress in their lifetimes, says a veterinary behaviourist.
- **Numbers:** "at least 50%"
- **Source:** Reisner I (DVM, PhD, DACVB). "Canine Separation Anxiety." *Today's Veterinary Practice*, Nov/Dec 2012.
- **URL:** https://todaysveterinarypractice.com/behavior/on-your-best-behavior-canine-separation-anxiety/
- **Proof:** "At least 50% of dogs, and probably more, will exhibit some aspect of separation distress in their lifetimes."
- **Confidence:** medium (expert clinical opinion in a trade journal, not a prevalence study)

### 1.7 RSPCA: many struggling dogs show no obvious sign
- **Deck line:** 8 in 10 dogs find it hard to cope alone; half show no obvious signs (RSPCA).
- **Source:** RSPCA, "Separation anxiety in dogs" advice page (accessed 2026).
- **URL:** https://www.rspca.org.uk/adviceandwelfare/pets/dogs/behaviour/separationrelatedbehaviour
- **Proof:** "Research suggests that 8 out of 10 dogs find it hard to cope when left alone. Yet, half won't show any obvious signs."
- **Confidence:** medium (major welfare body, but the page doesn't cite the underlying study). This line is useful for the pitch: owners can't see the problem, and a camera can.

### 1.8 India: new pet parents and the return to office
- **Deck line:** India's lockdown pet parents faced a return to office, and vets saw separation anxiety signs.
- **Numbers:** 20 million pet dogs in 2019, projected at 31 million by 2023 (as cited in the article). The article also cites an estimate that 400 million Indians would return to offices. It quotes an Indian vet on the signs: "waiting at the door, constant barking, crying out... chewing on household items."
- **Source:** Niharika Sharma, "India's new pet parents are anxious as work from home gradually comes to an end." Quartz India, republished by Scroll.in, 13 Jan 2021.
- **URL:** https://scroll.in/article/983773/indias-new-pet-parents-are-anxious-as-work-from-home-gradually-comes-to-an-end
- **Proof:** a pet parent quote: "starts crying even if I go out of his sight for 15 minutes." Vet SK Chaudhary lists the signs quoted above.
- **Confidence:** weak to medium (journalism, anecdotal; the numbers are second-hand)

## 2. Owner side

### 2.1 Guilt and cancelled plans (US)
- **Deck line:** 90% of pet owners feel guilty leaving pets alone; 73% have cancelled or cut plans short.
- **Numbers:** n=1,512 US pet owners, online, July 2025. 90% feel guilty. 73% cancelled or left plans early. 38% rearranged schedules or WFH days for pets. 46% use a pet camera or live feed.
- **Source:** Power Wizard, "What Americans Are Willing to Do to Keep Their Pets Happy," published 30 Sep 2025.
- **URL:** https://www.powerwizard.com/blog/how-far-americans-go-to-keep-their-pets-happy-at-home/
- **Proof:** "90% of pet owners feel guilty leaving their pets alone"; "73% have canceled or left plans early"; "38% of owners have rearranged their schedules or work from home (WFH) days for their pets"; "46% use a pet camera or live feed."
- **Confidence:** weak (a survey run by an energy-comparison brand for marketing; US only). The pattern matches Ravi's story, but label it as a brand survey on the slide.

### 2.2 Owners' own separation anxiety and willingness to trade salary (US)
- **Deck line:** 4 in 10 pet parents would take a lower salary to work from home with their pet.
- **Numbers:** n=2,000 US pet owners, OnePoll for PetMeds, Nov 2023. 40% would take lower pay to WFH. 44% worry about their pet's separation anxiety. 52% say their own separation anxiety has risen in the past two years. 48% say their pet follows them to the door and looks sad.
- **Source:** PetMed Express (PetMeds) press release, "Both Pets and Pet Parents Feel the Strain of Separation Anxiety When Apart," 27 Nov 2023.
- **URL:** https://s204.q4cdn.com/312753843/files/doc_news/Both-Pets-and-Pet-Parents-Feel-the-Strain-of-Separation-Anxiety-When-Apart-According-to-a-New-Survey-from-PetMeds-2023.pdf
- **Proof:** "four in 10 pet parents said they would take a lower salary to work from home in order to be closer to their pets"; "44% expressed worry about their pets experiencing separation anxiety."
- **Confidence:** weak (commissioned by a brand)

### 2.3 Independent poll: 40% feel anxious leaving pets alone all day
- **Deck line:** 40% of US pet owners feel anxious leaving pets home alone all day.
- **Source:** Hal Herzog, "Do You Suffer From Pet-Related Separation Anxiety?" *Psychology Today*, June 2022, citing a 2022 YouGov survey.
- **URL:** https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/blog/animals-and-us/202206/do-you-suffer-pet-related-separation-anxiety
- **Proof:** "In a 2022 YouGov survey, 40% of a representative sample of American pet owners answered yes to the question, 'Do you feel anxious leaving your pets at home alone all day?'"
- **Confidence:** medium (YouGov representative sample, read through a secondary source)

### 2.4 Travel and plans (TrustedHousesitters, n=5,000)
- **Deck line:** 56% of US pet owners travel less since getting a pet; 52% felt too guilty to go.
- **Numbers:** n=5,000 US pet owners, Opinion Matters, Mar to Apr 2025. 56% travel less. 52% say their pet's sad face while packing made them too guilty to go. 49% have separation anxiety themselves when away. 33% spend a lot of their vacation on their phones checking on their pet.
- **Source:** TrustedHousesitters press release (GlobeNewswire), 18 Jun 2025.
- **URL:** https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2025/06/18/3101671/0/en/Pets-Over-People-New-Survey-Reveals-Sacrifices-Americans-Make-For-Their-Pets.html
- **Proof:** "56% of U.S. pet owners say they travel less since getting a pet"; "A third (33%) of pet parents say they spend a significant portion of their vacation on their phones, checking in on their pet."
- **Confidence:** weak to medium (commissioned, but a large sample run by a named research firm)

### 2.5 Willingness to use cameras: less universal than it looks
- **Deck line:** Only 32% of US dog owners want a camera to check on their pet remotely.
- **Numbers:** YouGov, 2,223 US adults, Jan 2025. 32% of dog owners and 30% of cat owners are interested.
- **Source:** YouGov, "Most dog and cat owners say their pets know them at least as well as their friends and family," 2025.
- **URL:** https://yougov.com/en-us/articles/51499-most-dog-and-cat-owners-say-their-pets-know-them-well
- **Proof:** "Only 32% of people with dogs and 30% of people with cats express interest in using a camera to check on their pet remotely, if given the opportunity to do so."
- **Confidence:** medium. Use this honestly: plain "watch your dog" cameras have a ceiling. Dogify's pitch is alerts plus action, not watching.
- **Related:** among people who already own a camera, 38% check it during work and 94% call it one of the best parts of their day (Wakefield Research for Comcast/Xfinity, June 2019, https://corporate.comcast.com/press/releases/two-in-three-pet-owners-use-camera-to-check-in-on-pets). Confidence: weak (brand survey of existing camera users).

### 2.6 India pet-care market size and growth
- **Deck line:** India's pet-care spend doubled to $3.6B in FY24, heading to $7B by FY28.
- **Numbers:** $1.6B (FY19), $3.6B (FY24), $7.0 to 7.5B (FY28P). About 32M in-home pets in FY24 (about 26M in FY19), about 40M projected by FY28. - **Proof:** "India is home to ~32 Mn pets"; "Spending on petcare has grown 2x since FY20 to reach $3.6 Bn today"; "These pet parents spend ~₹50k yearly on petcare product and services"; "These spends account for 5-8% of their average household income."
- **Confidence:** medium (reputable consultancy, but the method is not fully disclosed)

- **Numbers:** more than 36.8M dogs in 2024 (Euromonitor). More than 51M projected by 2028 (Statista).

## 3. Signs of separation anxiety a camera or mic can catch

### 3.1 The first minutes matter most
- **Deck line:** Barking starts about 3 minutes after departure, destruction about 7 minutes after.
- **Numbers:** mean latency of 3.25 min for vocalisation and 7.13 min for destruction (Palestrini et al. 2010, video analysis), as cited in a peer-reviewed review.
- **Source:** Sargisson RJ. "Canine separation anxiety: strategies for treatment and management." *Veterinary Medicine: Research and Reports*, 2014, citing Palestrini C, Minero M, Cannas S, Rossi E, Frank D. "Video analysis of dogs with separation-related behaviors." *Applied Animal Behaviour Science* 124:61–67, 2010.
- **URL:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7521022/
- **Proof:** "the separation-related problem behaviors begin shortly after the departure of the owner – a latency of 3.25 minutes for vocalization and 7.13 minutes for destruction."
- **Confidence:** strong (primary video study, read through a review; the Palestrini paper itself was paywalled)

### 3.2 Within 30 minutes of departure (vet and welfare bodies)
- **Deck line:** Distress typically starts within 30 minutes of the owner leaving, when the owner can't see it.
- **Sources and proof:**
  - Reisner 2012 (*Today's Veterinary Practice*, link above): signs occur "typically immediately after, or within 30 minutes of, the owner's departure."
  - RSPCA (link above): signs appear "within a few to 30 minutes" of the owner leaving.
  - ASPCA, "Separation Anxiety": dogs begin "barking and displaying other distress behaviors within a short time after being left alone, often within minutes." https://www.aspca.org/pet-care/dog-care/common-dog-behavior-issues/separation-anxiety
  - Kenneth M. Martin DVM, DACVB, "Separation Anxiety: Home Alone," Canadian VMA proceedings, 2016: "For most pets anxiety builds as the attachment figure prepares to depart the home and it peaks within the first 20 minutes." https://www.canadianveterinarians.net/media/m0dds3pl/proceedings-2016-kenneth-martin-separation-anxiety.pdf
- **Confidence:** strong (consistent across several vet sources)

### 3.3 The observable signs, and where they happen
- **Deck line:** Barking, howling, pacing, panting, drooling, toileting, and chewing at doors: all visible or audible.
- **Signs listed by source:**
  - RSPCA: "Destructive behaviour... (often near exits, doors or windows)"; "Howling, barking, whining"; "Toileting"; "Trembling, panting"; "Excessive salivation"; self-injury; repetitive behaviour; vomiting; loss of appetite.
  - ASPCA: urinating and defecating, barking and howling, chewing, digging and destruction, escaping, pacing, coprophagia.
  - Reisner 2012: "Destructive chewing and digging, often at windows, doors, and other 'exit points'"; "Persistent vocalization."
  - VCA Animal Hospitals: "vocalization, destruction, or house soiling"; destruction "at the doors where owners depart... most often occurs shortly after departure." https://vcahospitals.com/know-your-pet/separation-anxiety-in-dogs
  - Harvey et al. 2022 (item 1.5) measured vocalisation, pacing or circling, destruction at doors and windows, and elimination.
  - de Assis et al. 2020 (item 1.2) found four clusters, including "Exit Frustration" (17.4%), with barrier-directed destruction and vocalisation at departure.
- **Confidence:** strong

### 3.4 Video is the diagnostic standard
- **Deck line:** Vets diagnose separation problems from home video; owners usually can't see the behaviour.
- **Proof:** Sargisson 2014: "Recording dog behavior using video cameras mounted in the home can be a useful way of both diagnosing separation-related disorders and of monitoring improvement." Martin 2016: a diagnosis is based on "history, presenting clinical signs, video/audio recording." RSPCA recommends "Installing a camera to monitor your pet while you're out."
- **Confidence:** strong. This supports Dogify's camera-first design directly.

### 3.5 A caution for the AI model: some anxious dogs go quiet
- **Deck line (internal, not for a slide):** Some anxious dogs freeze rather than bark, so a detector that only listens for barking misses them.
- **Source:** Pierantoni L, Albertini M, Piotti P, Ripamonti A, Pocar P, Borromeo V, Pirrone F. "Signs of Anxiety and Salivary Copeptin Levels in Dogs Diagnosed with Separation-Related Problems in a Short Separation Test." *Animals*, 2022 (n=28). https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9367405/
- **Proof:** the authors report that "case dogs were behaviorally inhibited while their owner was absent." RSPCA also notes that "half won't show any obvious signs."
- **Confidence:** medium (small sample)

## 4. What calms dogs, mapped to Dogify's 4 levers

### Lever A: Owner's voice (voice notes). Evidence: MIXED
**A1. Dog brains have voice-sensitive areas (the mechanism)**
- **Deck line:** Dog brains have dedicated voice areas that respond to human emotional tone (fMRI, 2014).
- **Source:** Andics A, Gácsi M, Faragó T, Kis A, Miklósi Á. "Voice-sensitive regions in the dog and human brain are revealed by comparative fMRI." *Current Biology* 24(5):574–578, 2014. (11 dogs in the scanner.)
- **URL:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24560578/
- **Proof:** "We demonstrate that voice areas exist in dogs... sensitivity to vocal emotional valence cues engages similarly located nonprimary auditory regions in dogs and humans."
- **Confidence:** strong as neuroscience, but it shows that dogs process voices. It does **not** show that a recorded voice calms a dog left alone.

**A2. A recorded owner voice lowered cortisol during separation (small study)**
- **Deck line:** A recording of the owner's voice lowered stress hormone in anxious dogs during separation (n=28).
- **Source:** Shin YJ, Shin NS. "Evaluation of effects of olfactory and auditory stimulation on separation anxiety by salivary cortisol measurement in dogs." *Journal of Veterinary Science* 17(2):153–158, 2016.
- **URL:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26645334/
- **Proof:** 28 dogs with separation anxiety, in 3 groups (control, owner's clothes, recording of owner's voice). Cortisol ratios "were significantly higher in group 1 [control] than in group 2 or 3... the owner's odor or voice may be helpful to managing stress in dogs with SA."
- **Confidence:** medium (small, 20-minute lab separation)

**A3. Owner-voice clips triggered by barking cut vocalisation (company-linked study)**
- **Deck line:** Owner-recorded voice feedback cut barking by 95.7% over two weeks in 40 dogs.
- **Source:** Tiira K. "Digital Dogsitter® reduces vocalization in dogs suffering from separation-related problems." *Applied Animal Behaviour Science* 243:105460, 2021. DOI 10.1016/j.applanim.2021.105460.
- **URLs:** https://researchportal.helsinki.fi/en/publications/digital-dogsitter-reduces-vocalization-in-dogs-suffering-from-sep/ ; the numbers come from https://www.digitaldogsitter.com/articles/research
- **Proof:** "A sample of 40 dogs and their owners participated in this study." "The reduction in vocalization after using Digital Dogsitter® was 95.7%, and the reduction in the number of loud events was 58.2%."
- **Confidence:** weak to medium. It is peer-reviewed, but the author runs a dog-behaviour company (smartDOG). The numbers were read on the product's own site, because the full paper was blocked. The summaries I could access describe a before/after design with no control group. It is the closest published analogue to Dogify's voice lever, and a direct competitor idea.

**A4. Counter-evidence: voice and audiobooks did not help separated dogs**
- **Deck line (honesty slide):** In two controlled studies, audiobooks and a friendly voice gave no extra calm during separation.
- **Sources and proof:**
  - Kinnaird RF, Wells DL. "The effect of auditory stimulation on pet dogs' reactions to owner separation." *Applied Animal Behaviour Science*, 2022 (60 + 22 dogs). https://pure.qub.ac.uk/en/publications/the-effect-of-auditory-stimulation-on-pet-dogs-reactions-to-owner/ . "Findings point to only a moderately calming effect of classical music, and no apparent welfare benefits of an audiobook, on dogs separated from their owners."
  - Flint HE, Atkinson M, Lush J, Hunt ABG, King T. "Long-Lasting Chews Elicit Positive Emotional States in Dogs during Short Periods of Social Isolation." *Animals*, 2023 (n=20). https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9951671/ . In this study a Furbo tossed kibble with and without a voice script: "the addition of a voice recording to the device throwing treats did not make a difference to engagement or ratings of the dog's emotional state." The authors note that the voices "were made by experimenters who had limited familiarity with the individual dogs." That leaves room for the *owner's* voice to work differently, but it has not been tested.
- **Confidence:** strong that generic voice has weak effects. Owner-specific voice at home remains an open question, and Dogify can test it.

### Lever B: Video on a screen facing the dog. Evidence: WEAK
**B1. Most dogs look at TV, but interest fades quickly**
- **Deck line:** Shelter dogs looked at TV only 10.8% of the time, and interest declined within days.
- **Source:** Graham L, Wells DL, Hepper PG. "The influence of visual stimulation on the behaviour of dogs housed in a rescue shelter." *Animal Welfare* 14:143–148, 2005 (n=50).
- **URL:** https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/animal-welfare/article/influence-of-visual-stimulation-on-the-behaviour-of-dogs-housed-in-a-rescue-shelter/6E849BA04353477890AE78F4E99E06AE
- **Proof:** "Dogs spent relatively little of the total observation time looking at the television monitors (10.8%)... their interest in all experimental conditions declined over time." On the positive side: "All of the experimental conditions encouraged significantly less vocalisation and movement than the control condition." They conclude dogs "may not benefit from this type of enrichment to the same degree as species with more well-developed visual systems."
- **Confidence:** medium (shelter dogs, not dogs at home alone)

**B2. Owners report that most dogs watch TV, especially footage of animals**
- **Deck line:** 88.3% of surveyed owners say their dog watches TV; animal content draws the strongest response.
- **Source:** Montgomery LI, Krichbaum S, Katz JS. "Characterizing TV viewing habits in companion dogs." *Scientific Reports*, 2025 (453 dogs analysed from 650 responses).
- **URL:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12271434/
- **Proof:** 88.3% of the original sample watched TV. The authors warn: "There was a self-selection of owners for those with dogs who do engage with television stimuli." There were no stress or welfare measures.
- **Confidence:** weak for calming. It shows engagement, not reduced anxiety.

**B3. Dog-to-owner video calling: one dog, inconclusive**
- **Deck line (internal):** A 2021 dog video-call prototype was tested on one dog; half its early calls were accidental.
- **Source:** Hirskyj-Douglas I et al., "Forming the Dog Internet: Prototyping a Dog-to-Human Video Call Device," ACM ISS 2021. University of Glasgow news, Nov 2021.
- **URL:** https://www.gla.ac.uk/news/archiveofnews/2021/november/headline_819757_en.html
- **Proof:** "Zack made 18 calls, half of which were 'accidental' calls." The researcher says "we can't know for sure that Zack was aware of the causal link."
- **Confidence:** weak (n=1)
- **Takeaway:** position video as the owner's choice with an unproven effect, not as a validated calming tool. VCA does list "Leave the TV on. Play a favorite video or some music" as practical advice.

### Lever C: Food, treats, food puzzles, and counter-conditioning. Evidence: MODERATE, with an important catch
**C1. Vet bodies recommend a food toy at departure (counter-conditioning)**
- **Deck line:** ASPCA advises a food puzzle lasting 20 to 30 minutes every time you leave.
- **Source:** ASPCA, "Separation Anxiety" (link above).
- **Proof:** "every time you leave the house, you can offer your dog a puzzle toy stuffed with food that will take him at least 20 to 30 minutes to finish." Counterconditioning is "associating the sight or presence of a feared... situation with something really good." Reisner 2012: food toys "distract and countercondition... redirect the dog's attention to something pleasant during an unpleasant time."
- **Confidence:** strong as standard vet guidance. The 20 to 30 minute window lines up with the peak-distress window in 3.2.

**C2. Food-stuffed toys increase activity and reduce barking (kennel dogs)**
- **Deck line:** A treat-stuffed Kong raised activity and lowered barking in kennelled dogs.
- **Source:** Schipper LL, Vinke CM, Schilder MBH, Spruijt BM. "The effect of feeding enrichment toys on the behaviour of kennelled dogs (Canis familiaris)." *Applied Animal Behaviour Science* 114, 2008 (pilot, 8 dogs + 9 controls).
- **URL:** https://happydogtraining.info/wp-content/uploads/studies/The-Effect-of-Feeding-Enrichment-Toys-on-the-Behaviour-of-Kennelled-Dogs-Canis-Familiaris-by-Schipper-et-al-2008.pdf
- **Proof:** "the presence of these food enrichment toys stimulates appetitive behaviours and increases the level of activity... Moreover, it also appears to result into lower barking frequencies."
- **Confidence:** medium (small pilot in lab kennels)

**C3. Remote treat tossing is the weakest food format tested**
- **Deck line:** A long-lasting chew beat a Furbo treat-tosser for calm engagement during 20 minutes alone.
- **Source:** Flint et al. 2023 (item A4).
- **Proof:** "Dogs spent the most time interacting with a long-lasting chew, and responded to this enrichment with the most positive and calm emotions when compared to a treat-dispensing toy and a smart treat-dispensing device." With voice added, time engaged with the device was about 3 min 8 s out of 20.
- **Confidence:** medium (n=20, lab room, sponsor-affiliated authors: Pedigree/Mars). **This matters for the deck:** a single remote treat toss is weak enrichment. Dogify's case for the treat lever is *timing*: rewarding calm at the right moment, rather than entertainment.

**C4. Counter-conditioning helps, but separation problems resist it**
- **Deck line:** A 2024 systematic review found counter-conditioning works, but separation problems are the most resistant.
- **Source:** Shnookal J, Tepper D, Howell T, Bennett P. "Counterconditioning-based interventions for companion dog behavioural modification: A systematic review." *Applied Animal Behaviour Science*, 2024. DOI 10.1016/j.applanim.2024.106305 (abstract read via OpenAlex).
- **Proof:** "The majority of the studies resulted in positive outcomes... However, separation-related behaviours were found to be more resistant to change."
- **Confidence:** strong (systematic review, 14 studies)

**C5. The catch: highly anxious dogs often won't eat alone**
- **Proof:** ASPCA: "highly anxious dogs usually won't eat when their guardians aren't home." VCA lists "refusal to eat" as a sign.
- **Confidence:** strong (vet body guidance). **Product implication:** a treat the dog ignores is itself a signal of anxiety that the AI can log.

### Lever D: Toys and play enrichment. Evidence: WEAK to MODERATE
**D1. Dogs lose interest in the same toy quickly, and novelty resets it**
- **Deck line:** Dogs habituate to a toy within minutes; a different toy restores interest.
- **Source:** Pullen AJ, Merrill RJ, Bradshaw JWS. "Habituation and dishabituation during object play in kennel-housed dogs." *Animal Cognition* 15(6):1143–1150, 2012 (16 Labradors).
- **URL:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22825035/
- **Proof:** "Domestic dogs are reported to show intense but transient neophilia towards novel objects... loss of interest in the object... is due to habituation to the overall stimulus properties of the toy."
- **Confidence:** medium. This supports rolling out a *favourite or rotated* toy on demand rather than leaving one lying around.

**D2. Giving toys alone didn't improve welfare in pet dogs**
- **Deck line (honesty):** Twelve days of added toys didn't change pet dogs' activity, appetite, or mood.
- **Source:** Chan K, Arellano C, Horowitz A. "Here Puppy, Chew on This: Short-Term Provision of Toys Does Not Improve Welfare in Companion Dogs." *Animals*, 2023. DOI 10.3390/ani13213340 (abstract read via Europe PMC).
- **Proof:** "provisioning companion dogs with toys did not significantly alter their activity level, rate of food consumption, or cognitive bias."
- **Confidence:** medium

### Supporting audio evidence (music and audiobooks)
**E1. Classical music: more sleep, less barking (kennels)**
- **Deck line:** Classical music made 117 kennelled dogs sleep more and bark less; heavy metal increased shaking.
- **Source:** Kogan LR, Schoenfeld-Tacher R, Simon AA. "Behavioral effects of auditory stimulation on kenneled dogs." *Journal of Veterinary Behavior* 7:268–275, 2012.
- **URL:** https://www.rescueanimalmp3.org/wp-content/uploads/2012/10/Behavioral-effects-of-auditory-stimulation-on-kenneled-dogs-published1.pdf
- **Proof:** "classical music leads to kenneled dogs spending more time sleeping... and less time vocalizing... Heavy metal music... appears to increase body shaking."
- **Confidence:** strong for shelter dogs

**E2. Audiobooks: more resting than music (kennels)**
- **Deck line:** Audiobooks beat classical and pop music for resting time in 31 shelter dogs.
- **Source:** Brayley C, Montrose VT. "The effects of audiobooks on the behaviour of dogs at a rehoming kennels." *Applied Animal Behaviour Science* 174:111–115, 2016.
- **URL:** https://caninewelfare.centers.purdue.edu/resource/the-effects-of-audiobooks-on-the-behaviour-of-dogs-at-a-rehoming-kennels
- **Proof:** "audiobooks resulted in dogs spending more of their time resting than when exposed to any of the other auditory conditions."
- **Confidence:** medium. **But** see Kinnaird & Wells 2022 (A4): in *pet dogs separated from their owners*, the audiobook gave "no apparent welfare benefits." Kennel results don't transfer cleanly to separation at home.

### The key warning: reward calm, not barking
- **Deck line:** Fussing over "bad" behaviour made puppies 6 times more likely to develop separation problems.
- **Sources and proof:**
  - Dale et al. 2024 (item 1.1): "Puppies whose owners reported 'fussing' over their dogs at six months in response to 'bad' behaviour upon their return... were six times more likely to display SRBs." Confidence: medium (association, not proof of cause; n=145).
  - VCA Animal Hospitals: "attention-seeking behavior be ignored"; a "settled response achieves rewards and attention seeking does not"; teach that "calm and quiet behavior is the only way to receive attention." Confidence: strong (vet guidance).
  - ASPCA: on arrival, "don't pay any more attention to him until he's calm and relaxed." ASPCA and RSPCA both warn never to punish.
- **Nuance to state honestly:** behaviourists distinguish *emotion* from *behaviour*. Comforting a scared dog is not generally thought to "reinforce fear". What can be reinforced is an *operant behaviour* such as attention-seeking barking. So Dogify's rule, "treat and voice go to calm, not to barking", is built for barking and demand behaviour. It does not mean ignoring a dog in panic. This nuance came from trainer blogs and was not verified in a peer-reviewed source, so treat it as a design principle, not a stat. It is also a tension with Digital Dogsitter (A3), which plays the owner's voice *in response to* barking.
